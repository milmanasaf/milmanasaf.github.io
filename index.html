About
"Journalism is the first draft of history — and every story begins with curiosity."

Welcome to my journalism portfolio. I'm Asaf Milman, a staff writer for both The Campanile and Verde Magazine, the student publications of Palo Alto High School. This portfolio documents my work, growth, and contributions across a range of beats — from local government and education to science, environment, and community issues.

My reporting this semester has taken me from the streets of East Palo Alto to the halls of Paly, covering stories that matter to my community. I believe journalism's power lies in specificity: the right quote, the right source, the right detail that makes a story come alive.

Minor Reflections

Category Commentaries

~100-word reflections for each JEA portfolio category, Spring 2026.

Reflection 1 — Writing & Reporting

~100 words

This semester I published seven articles across The Campanile and Verde Magazine, covering beats from local government to science and opinion. My East Palo Alto homicide story was challenging, as I had to find expert sources outside the school community, which took a lot of emails because I was ghosted and ignored multiple times. My community college opinion piece was especially interesting to me because it was going against common perceptions from our community. Across these pieces I learned to balance speed with accuracy, finding sources under deadline pressure, and writing in different formats: news, feature, editorial, and spotlight.

Reflection 2 — Web & Social Media

~100 words

Beyond print I also have made 2 social media stories which have shown me what it's like to write articles under an even stricter deadline and take even more initiative. First, I had to learn how to take good photos, and had to interview people on the spot. I had two of my stories cut because I did not get enough interviews or write my stories fast enough, so it really taught me not to procrastinate and how to write stories in less time. The word limit also taught me how to trim down stories and recognize which parts and interviews are more important than others.

Reflection 3 — Law, Ethics & News Literacy

~100 words

For law and ethics my story on East Palo Alto's decrease in crime was really eye-opening. I learned how to reach out to many different types of sources like locals, professionals, and lawmakers/government officials. It was also the first news story I had written and taught me how to order quotes and ideas properly without being repetitive or including unimportant facts. On another hand, I had to approach the story from many different points of view as it has been a widely misunderstood topic, so I had to balance local opinions and professional opinions.

Reflection 4 — Design

~100 words

Coming into the semester, I did not know how to design pages or properly operate InDesign. I had to reach out to my editors in chief and ask for help with basics and even just starting. I also had to work with different people each cycle which showed me how to communicate effectively with others about timing and sharing work. In the previous cycle, the editorials page, which I was designing, took longer since the editorials were finished late. This helped me figure out tricks and shortcuts on InDesign to finish it faster since the due date was near. Ultimately I was proud of my designs over the semester and felt small improvements each cycle.

Reflection 5 — Commitment to Diversity

~100 words

The story I wrote this semester that I was most proud of and was most important to diversity in my community was the story about antisemitism. In it I explored misconceptions about ideas that I believe in like Zionism, and also explored the causes of antisemitism which has been on the rise recently. This story was also important to me because I had to step out of my comfort zone in releasing opinions that I normally wouldn't because of fear of backlash from the community. It was very fulfilling to hear back from members of the Jewish community, and family members about how much the story mattered to them and how happy they were to see it.

Major Reflection — Semester Self-Assessment

500 words minimum · Spring 2026

Being a part of the Campanile has shaped how I express my opinion more carefully and also how I look at everyday situations. I have recently started exploring many different perspectives to topics I am interested in and topics that I only thought of in one way before. Being a staff writer has also shaped my time management skills because I was not used to having a deadline for a group, and being late to something in the Campanile would not just affect myself but also others on the staff.

One of my biggest struggles was writing my story on antisemitism. I have such a strong opinion on the topic and I really had to broaden and almost self-censor because so many people think I have antisemitic ideas. Antisemitism has been a part of my whole life, and summing it up in one story was super difficult for me. Even framing the story as an opinion was so hard for me because it makes it that much easier to dismiss as wrong or biased, even though I had real facts to back it up. Another challenge I faced was balancing Campanile with my other obligations such as soccer, which I often had practices for during production. I had to decide which was more important and skip production sometimes while skipping soccer in others.

The most direct transfer of skills has been in communication. Cold-emailing professors, coordinating with officials, and interviewing strangers on the spot for social media stories all required me to communicate professionally with adults in a way most high schoolers never have to. That confidence has shown up outside class in how I write emails, how I introduce myself, and how I approach unfamiliar situations without hesitation.

Non-bylined work I have done this semester included spending time on design and stories late because other members of the staff took longer on their ends. One of my partners for the spotlight we wrote procrastinated a lot of their work, so I had to reach out on my own and put in more time to design and write the story. Another example is designing an editorial page late because the story was behind schedule.

A moment that was super important for me was during my time writing a story about East Palo Alto and just taking time to get to know and understand a resident that I hadn't known before. Another moment was hearing feedback on my story about antisemitism and how much it meant to members of the Palo Alto Jewish community and members of my own family — it made me feel like what I wrote actually matters and impacts people.

I think currently journalism as a whole is flawed. Publications are racing to see who can get information out first instead of who can release more factual and unbiased information that can educate people. There have been times where famous publications had to edit or take down stories because they used incorrect facts, which wouldn't have happened if they spent more time writing.
